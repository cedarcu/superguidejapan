Foreigner Housing Guide: Zero Key Money & Guarantors

Renting an apartment in Japan as a foreigner can be challenging due to high initial costs and strict guarantor requirements. Here is how to navigate the system.

Understanding Initial Costs

Initial move-in costs can easily equal 4 to 5 months of rent. Watch out for:

  • Reikin (Key Money / 礼金): A non-refundable "gift" to the landlord. (Try to avoid apartments with this).
  • Shikikin (Security Deposit / 敷金): Usually 1-2 months rent. Partially refunded after cleaning fees when you move out.
  • Brokerage Fee (仲介手数料): Paid to the real estate agent (usually 0.5 to 1 month rent).
  • Guarantor Company Fee (保証会社): 50% to 100% of one month's rent.

Foreigner-Friendly Housing Options

  • UR Housing (UR賃貸住宅): Government-subsidized housing.
    Pros: No key money, no agency fee, no renewal fee, and NO guarantor required.
    Cons: You must prove a stable income, and locations might be further from city centers.
  • Village House (ビレッジハウス): Renovated public housing run by a private company.
    Pros: Very cheap rent, zero upfront key money/deposit/agency fees, highly foreigner-friendly.
    Cons: Buildings are older, thin walls, and locations are often suburban.
  • Share Houses (Oakhouse, Sakura House): Great for initial landing.
    Pros: Fully furnished, utilities and internet included, no guarantors, very low initial costs.
