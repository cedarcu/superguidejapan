Budget Shopping Guide: 100-Yen Shops & Discount Groceries

100-Yen Shops (百均 - Hyakkin)

Your best friends for setting up a new apartment on a budget. Everything is 100 JPY (110 JPY with tax) unless marked otherwise.

  • Daiso (ダイソー): The biggest chain. Great for kitchenware, cleaning supplies, and storage boxes.
  • Seria (セリア): Known for more stylish, cute, and craft-oriented items. Excellent tableware.
  • Can Do (キャンドゥ): Great for basic necessities and unique food items.

Second-Hand Chains (リサイクルショップ)

Japan's second-hand goods are notoriously high quality and well-maintained.

  • Hard Off / Book Off: Best for used electronics, musical instruments, games, and books.
  • 2nd Street (セカンドストリート): Excellent for used clothing, furniture, and home appliances.
  • Jimoty (ジモティー): A local classifieds app (like Craigslist). You can often find free or extremely cheap furniture from people moving out, but you usually have to pick it up yourself.

Discount Grocery Stores

  • Gyomu Super (業務スーパー): The absolute best for bulk buying, frozen vegetables, and cheap imported meats (including Halal options).
  • Don Quijote (ドン・キホーテ): Mega discount store. Great for snacks, cosmetics, and cheap daily necessities, especially late at night.
  • Local Supermarkets after 7 PM: Look for the discount stickers (割引 - Waribiki) or half-price stickers (半額 - Hangaku) on bento boxes and fresh food before closing.
