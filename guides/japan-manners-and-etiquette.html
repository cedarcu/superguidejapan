Essential Japan Manners & Rules: The First-Timer's Complete Do's & Don'ts Checklist (日本のマナーとエチケット)

Japan is famous for its politeness, order, and social harmony. For a smooth trip or transition into living here, it’s crucial to respect the unwritten (and sometimes written) rules of daily life.

✅ Top 10 Golden Rules Checklist

  • 1. Phones strictly on silent mode (Manner Mode) on trains.
  • 2. No loud talking or phone calls on public transport.
  • 3. Do not leave tips at restaurants.
  • 4. Never stick chopsticks vertically in rice.
  • 5. Take your trash home; public bins are rare.
  • 6. No eating while walking on busy streets.
  • 7. No smoking on streets except in designated smoking booths.
  • 8. Remove shoes when entering homes (and some restaurants).
  • 9. Wear specific toilet slippers when in the bathroom.
  • 10. Stand on the correct side of escalators (Left in Tokyo, Right in Osaka).

1. Public Transport & Train Manners

  • Manner Mode (マナーモード): Always set your phone to silent. Strictly NO phone calls inside trains or buses. If you must speak, whisper quietly.
  • Backpack Etiquette: Carry your backpack on your chest or place it on the overhead racks, especially during rush hour.
  • Priority Seating (優先席 - Yusenseki): Give up these seats for the elderly, pregnant women, and disabled. Turn off your phone near priority seats when the train is crowded to avoid interfering with pacemakers.
  • Escalators: Stand on the left in Tokyo/Kanto. Stand on the right in Kansai/Osaka. Leave the other side open for people walking.

2. Garbage Disposal & Public Streets

  • Strict Garbage Sorting: Separate your trash into Burnable (燃えるゴミ), Non-burnable (燃えないゴミ), PET bottles, aluminum cans, and glass bottles.
  • No Public Trash Cans: You must take your trash home. Convenience store bins are for customers only; use them responsibly.
  • Smoking Bans: Street smoking is strictly banned and heavily fined (歩きタバコ禁止 - Aruki-tabako ban). Only smoke in designated enclosed smoking booths.
  • Eating While Walking (食べ歩き - Tabearuki): It is generally considered rude and messy. Stop at a bench or stand near the stall where you bought the food.

3. Dining & Restaurant Etiquette

  • Chopstick Taboos: NEVER pass food chopstick-to-chopstick (Hashi-watashi), as it resembles a funeral ritual. Never stick chopsticks vertically into rice (Tsukitate-bashi).
  • No Tipping (チップ不要): Tipping is not practiced in Japan. If you leave money on the table, staff will chase you down the street thinking you forgot your change.
  • Otōshi (お通し): In izakayas (pubs), you will be served a small mandatory appetizer. This is a standard table charge (often 300-500 JPY), not a scam.

4. Shoes & Indoors (Genkan) Etiquette

  • Taking Off Shoes: Always remove your shoes at the entrance (Genkan) of homes, ryokans, and certain restaurants. Step directly into the house/slippers, avoiding stepping in socks on the dirty genkan floor.
  • Shoe Placement: Turn your shoes to face outwards toward the door so they are easy to slip into when leaving.
  • Toilet Slippers: Use the specific slippers provided inside the restroom, and remember to swap back when you exit!

5. Apartment Living & Neighbor Etiquette

  • Noise Consciousness: Japanese apartment walls can be very thin. Walk softly, keep TV volumes low, and do not run washing machines or vacuums after 9:00 PM.
  • Community Rules: Follow the local garbage calendar meticulously. Greet your neighbors politely when you see them in the halls.
