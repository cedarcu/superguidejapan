First Days in Japan: SIM, Banking & City Hall Registration

Your first 14 days in Japan involve a specific sequence of administrative tasks. You cannot get a bank account without a registered address, and you cannot get a standard phone contract without a bank account.

Step 1: City Hall Registration (Juminhyo)

Within 14 days of moving into your permanent address, you must register it at your local municipal office (City Hall / Ward Office).

  • Bring your Passport and Residence Card (Zairyu Card).
  • The staff will print your address on the back of your Residence Card.
  • Ask them to print a Juminhyo (Certificate of Residence) for about 300 JPY. You will need this for banking and mobile contracts.

Step 2: Getting a Local Phone Number (SIM)

Major carriers (Docomo, Softbank) require a bank account to sign up. Therefore, you must use a foreigner-friendly MVNO (Mobile Virtual Network Operator) that accepts foreign credit cards or convenience store payments initially.

  • GTN Mobile: Highly recommended for new arrivals. Multilingual support, accepts overseas credit cards and convenience store payments.
  • Mobal: Can be picked up at the airport, operates on the Softbank network, accepts foreign cards.
  • Rakuten Mobile: Great once you have a Japanese bank account or credit card. Unlimited data options and point earning.

Step 3: Opening a Bank Account

If you have been in Japan for less than 6 months, traditional banks (SMBC, MUFG) will reject you. Your best options are:

  • Yucho Bank (Japan Post Bank): The easiest bank to open an account with on day one. You just need your registered Residence Card and Hanko (personal seal - though signatures are becoming accepted at some branches).
  • Shinsei Bank: Very foreigner-friendly, English online banking, but strict about the 6-month residency rule.
  • Sony Bank: Excellent English support and an English app. Comes with a good debit card, but also requires 6 months of residency.
